A History of the Crisis

The Anatomy of Exposure

At Signal Earth, our investigation began with an unyielding mandate: to dissect seven years of continuous ambient telemetry from Delhi's northern corridor. What the instruments recorded was not an occasional spike, but an enduring chronic baseline.

Measuring under 2.5 microns across, fine particulate matter ( PM2.5 ) bypasses upper respiratory cilia, migrating past cellular barriers into pulmonary gas-exchange alveoli and entering systemic blood circulation. Over 62.6% of all monitored days breached national safety thresholds, while 78.8% violated acceptable air quality limits.

The Airshed Knows No Borders

Our investigation reveals that Delhi's crisis cannot be understood through city limits alone. Bounded by the towering Himalayan mountain barrier to the north, the entire Indo-Gangetic Basin functions as a single enclosed atmospheric bowl.

During post-monsoon and winter months, cold ground-level radiation inversions trap transboundary agricultural residue smoke, industrial emissions, and urban vehicular exhaust in a stagnant planetary boundary layer less than 300 meters above ground.
