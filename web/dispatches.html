POLICY ASSESSMENT · MARCH 2024

CPCB National Clean Air Programme (NCAP): Regional Airshed Modeling Requirements

Five years after the launch of India's National Clean Air Programme (NCAP), review assessments across 131 non-attainment cities indicate mixed outcomes. While particulate monitoring density has expanded significantly, atmospheric modeling confirms that administrative municipal limits cover less than 15% of the total airshed area driving winter ground-level concentrations in the Indo-Gangetic basin.

Why this matters for North India: Municipal interventions (such as mechanical road sweeping, localized construction bans, or traffic rationing within city limits) cannot resolve transboundary agricultural biomass smoke and industrial transport that pool south of the Himalayan barrier. Regulatory targets must transition from isolated city-action plans to coordinated inter-state airshed mandates.

Source: Central Pollution Control Board / MoEFCC (National Clean Air Programme Review, 2024)
ATMOSPHERIC CHEMISTRY · NOVEMBER 2023

IIT Kanpur Air Quality Study: Precursor Gases & Secondary Aerosol Mass

Chemical speciation studies conducted by researchers at the Indian Institute of Technology (IIT) Kanpur reveal that primary combustion soot is only a partial component of winter particulate spikes. Secondary inorganic aerosols—primarily ammonium sulfate and ammonium nitrate formed from atmospheric reactions between agricultural ammonia (NH3) and industrial/vehicular nitrogen oxides (NOx)—frequently account for over 35–40% of total PM2.5 mass during inversion episodes.

Why this matters for North India: Standard mitigation strategies that focus exclusively on physical dust suppression overlook the chemical reactions occurring in the stagnant boundary layer. Controlling gaseous ammonia and nitrogen oxides is equally critical to reducing ground-level PM2.5 peaks.

Source: Indian Institute of Technology Kanpur (Department of Civil & Environmental Engineering, 2023)
EPIDEMIOLOGY · AUGUST 2023

The Lancet Planetary Health: Chronic Particulate Baseline Exposure in the Gangetic Plain

Epidemiological modeling across northern Indian cohorts demonstrates that chronic exposure to ambient PM2.5 concentrations sustained above 90–100 µg/m³ during winter months contributes significantly to cardiovascular morbidity and pediatric respiratory impairment. The study notes that seasonal surges compound the health burden beyond what annual average thresholds indicate.

Why this matters for North India: Station telemetry in Kanpur and Delhi frequently records 5-fold seasonal jumps between August monsoon lows and November peaks. Accurately tracking these extreme temporal swings is vital for informing public health advisories and hospital capacity planning.

Source: The Lancet Planetary Health (Volume 7, Issue 8, 2023)
